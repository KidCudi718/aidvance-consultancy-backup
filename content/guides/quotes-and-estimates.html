If quoting happens after dinner, you are not disorganised and you are not slow. You are doing the same assembly job by hand, from a standing start, every single time, and assembly is the part that does not have to be yours.

Separate the thinking from the typing

Pull apart what actually happens when you put a quote together. In every trade and every professional service it splits the same way.

The thinking. How complicated is this job really. What is this particular customer like. Where is the risk. Is this a job worth having. What is the number. That judgement is the whole value of you, it came from years of doing this, and it should never leave your hands.

The typing. Pulling last month's similar quote to copy the structure. Looking up the line items. Formatting it. Writing the same covering note. Checking you have not left the previous client's name in. Chasing it a week later because you heard nothing.

Now count honestly. In most businesses the thinking is ten or fifteen minutes and the typing is forty-five. The typing is where your evenings go, and the typing is the part a machine is genuinely good at.

Build the skeleton once

Take your last ten quotes and put them side by side. You are looking for what is identical every time, what changes predictably, and what is genuinely bespoke.

  • Identical every time: your terms, what is included, what is not, payment arrangements, your guarantee, how to accept.
  • Changes predictably: the line items, the quantities, the timing, the price.
  • Genuinely bespoke: the paragraph explaining what you understood about their job, and why you have approached it the way you have.

The first group gets written properly once and never again. The second group gets a structure. The third is the only part that deserves your attention from a blank page, and it is also the part customers actually read, which is why it is worth having the time back to write it well.

The single highest-value paragraph in any quote

Three or four sentences at the top proving you listened: what they told you, what you understood the real problem to be, and what you would do about it. Most quotes in most industries do not have this, which is exactly why it wins work.

When people say a quote “feels expensive”, they usually mean it arrived as a number with no evidence that anyone had thought about them.

Where the assistant fits

Four places, in the order worth trying them.

Turning your notes into the understanding paragraph

You come back from a site visit with scrappy notes or a voice memo. Hand it over with your skeleton and ask for that opening paragraph in your voice. You edit it, and editing three sentences is a different activity from writing them at nine at night.

Catching what you left out

Paste in a finished quote along with your own checklist of what a complete quote contains, and ask what is missing. It is unglamorous and it catches the omission that costs you money about once a month.

Writing the follow-up you never send

Most quotes are lost to silence rather than to price. A short, non-pestering follow-up a week later is the highest-return activity in the whole process and nobody does it, because writing it feels like begging. Draft five at a time, read them, send them.

Rewriting the same explanation for a different reader

The version for a homeowner and the version for a facilities manager are not the same document. That is a translation job, and translation is something these tools are unusually good at.

Be careful here

Quoting touches money, so two rules are not optional.

  • Never let it generate a number. Not a price, not a quantity, not a total. It will produce something plausible and plausible is the worst possible failure mode when a customer is going to sign it. Numbers come from you or from your own price list, every time.
  • Read every quote before it leaves. All of it, including the parts you did not change. The most common embarrassment by a distance is a previous customer's name or a previous job's detail surviving into the new document.

What good looks like

The realistic end state is not that quotes write themselves. It is that a quote takes twenty minutes instead of an hour, the twenty minutes is the part that needed your judgement, and the follow-up actually goes out.

That last one tends to be where the money is. Businesses that start following up consistently usually find the win rate moves more than anything else they changed.
